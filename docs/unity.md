# Playing the current build

1. Clone the repository and open the folder in Unity Hub with Unity 6 LTS (any 6000.0 release; accept the version prompt).
2. Open any scene, or make a new empty one. Press Play.
3. You are on *Sparrow*, moored at Hopwas bridge on the real canal, pointing south towards Fazeley, with the 1900 one-inch map laid on the ground underneath. W or up is ahead, S or down is astern, A and D or the arrows are the rudder. The boat only answers the helm once it has steerage way.
4. Ahead carries you round the canal's own bends: the helm steers you across the water, and the canal carries you along it, past Whittington, through Hopwas, round Fazeley Junction and over the Tame into Tamworth. The stop-planks at Huddlesford and the closed bottom gates of Glascote locks hold you with your bow against them, and a chain closes the Birmingham and Fazeley where it leaves Fazeley Junction. That is the pound, 12.8 kilometres of level water, and the whole of the demo.
5. The canal's sides change as you go. Most of the way the banks are grass and the towpath, on your left heading south, is a dirt path between grass verges. Through Hopwas, and from above Fazeley Junction all the way into Tamworth, it is paved behind a stone edge. Seven mooring spots have a hard edge and bollards to tie up to: below Huddlesford Junction, by Whittington, under Hopwas Hays Wood, either side of Hopwas bridge, above Fazeley Junction, and below Glascote locks. They are listed by name, as distances from those landmarks, in `Assets/Game/Pound/PoundStretches.cs`.

The game logic lives in `Assets/Game` and knows nothing about Unity. `Assets/Unity` connects it to the scene, and builds the whole pound at runtime so no scene file has to be kept in step with the code yet. The map it builds from is in `Assets/StreamingAssets/map/`, generated by `python3 -m tools.map` (see `docs/map/README.md`). The map is drawn by hand off the 1900 map (`python3 -m tools.map --traced`): the canal, the Tame, the railways, the main roads, the woods and the cottages of Whittington, Hopwas and Fazeley.
