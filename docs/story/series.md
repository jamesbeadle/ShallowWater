# The series

Shallow Water is an invented history of Britain from 1938 to 1958. It is about revolution and power. Over twenty years, Arthur Granger goes from a gun for hire on an old canal boat to the man who leads the country. He fixes what the men in charge would not fix, and the series ends with Britain as a country that works for the people in it. That ending is a *what could have been*. Every chapter makes him pay for it, because he gets there by doing bad things for the greater good.

## The title

The series is **Shallow Water**. The demo is *Locked*. The games after it are numbered, and each takes its title from the water it opens or the ground it takes:

| Chapter | Title | Ground |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Demo | *Shallow Water: Locked* | Huddlesford to Fazeley, the strike, autumn 1938 |
| 2 | *Shallow Water: The Grand Union* | Birmingham and the Grand Union to the edge of London, 1938 to 1939 |
| 3 | *Shallow Water: The Tidal Thames* | A canal boat on a tidal river: the docks, the estuary, Westminster seen from the water, the war declared, 1939 |
| 4 | *Shallow Water 4* (working title) | The war on the water, 1940 to 1945 |
| 5 | *Shallow Water 5* (working title) | The peace that was promised, 1945 to 1950 |
| 6 | *Shallow Water 6* (working title) | The rising, 1950 to 1955 |
| 7 | *Shallow Water 7* (working title) | Westminster, 1955 to 1958 |

A chapter gets its name once its water and its ground are known. Until then it has a number.

## What the title means

Shallow water means three things, and every chapter should show all three.

1. **The literal water.** A neglected canal silts up. Locks nobody tends start to leak, the pounds drain and boats ground on the mud. In 1938 the cut is losing to the road, and it gets shallower every year. The men who let it silt are the men who are letting the country silt too. When Granger takes power, the water comes back.
2. **The work.** Bounty work, recovery and killing for money sit in the margin between the law and the crime. You can wade into it, but you cannot see the bottom. Politics turns out to be the same water, only wider.
3. **The man.** Granger stays shallow on purpose and lets nobody see how deep he goes. The war is down there somewhere, and so is how far he will go. The country takes him for a plain man off a boat, and that is how he wins it.

## The shape of the series

The model is a long-running book series, not a trilogy. It has one protagonist, one world and one spine, and every chapter has its own self-contained job.

- **Each chapter closes its own case.** The demo's deserter and Game 2's guns are jobs the player is hired for, and when the player finishes one it is finished.
- **Each chapter takes him one rung higher.** Gun for hire, then a man with friends on the cut, then a name the papers print, then an outlaw the people protect, then the man in charge. Every rung costs something he cannot get back.
- **Each chapter makes a friend and an enemy who last.** The people he meets come back years later, older, and owing him or out to get him.
- **Each chapter has at least one twist the player did not see coming.** Some of them are shocks: a friend dies, an ally turns, a choice made two chapters earlier comes back. See [the-long-story.md](the-long-story.md).
- **Each chapter tells one true thing about the war.** Granger does not volunteer war stories, so the series does not either. Each chapter gives one fact, earned and never shown as a flashback, until the chapter that tells the whole of it.
- **Each chapter opens more of the map.** The map only ever grows, and water opened once stays open. The canals run through the back of every town, so a man who owns the canals can reach the whole country.
- **Time moves.** Each chapter is dated. Characters age, die, marry and leave the cut for the road. The wireless says what year it is.

## Rules every chapter keeps

1. Granger always does what he believes is right for the country, and it is often wrong for the person in front of him. The game never tells the player whether he was right.
2. Every rise costs a bad thing done: a killing, a betrayal, a lie told to someone who trusted him. The player chooses how and to whom, but never whether.
3. He works for whoever pays until he no longer needs to, and then he uses each side's money against them.
4. Nobody explains the past. Anyone who tries is lying or selling.
5. Violence is short and ugly, and it costs a favour. A shot fired on the towpath is heard for seven miles, and a shot fired in London is heard everywhere.
6. The boat families are the memory of the cut, and later they are the backbone of what he builds. Cross them and the world closes its doors, chapter after chapter.
7. The wireless, the pubs and the towpath gossip carry the real history of the year. The invented history leaves real history at the end of the war and never winks at the player about it.
8. A new chapter may kill anyone but Granger. *Sparrow* is moored outside Parliament in the last chapter, and the series is the story of how she got there.
