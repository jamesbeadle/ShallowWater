# The map: what lies along the water

Read from the period map in `docs/map/`. North to south, Huddlesford to Fazeley, with everything within about a mile of the bank. Distances are from the canal.

## Huddlesford Junction (the north gate)

- **The junction** itself: the Coventry Canal meets the Wyrley and Essington arm west to Lichfield. A toll cottage, a bridge, a wharf. The arm is the dead water.
- **The Plough**, the inn at the junction on the road bridge.
- **The London and North Western Railway**, the Trent Valley line, crosses the canal here and runs beside it, east of the water, all the way to Tamworth. Trains every hour are the pound's clock.
- **Streethay and Lichfield Trent Valley junction**, a mile north-west: the road and rail way into Lichfield, the way a stranger comes.
- **Hill Farm, Hurst Farm, Whittington Hurst, Thatchmoor**: the farms north of the water. Open, flat, no cover.
- **Fisherwick Park** and **Fisherwick Park Farm**, a mile east: the ghost of a demolished hall, old parkland, the River Tame beyond with **Elford** and its mill and weir on the far bank.

## Whittington

- **The village**, half a mile south of the canal: church, the Hall, the Old Hall, a smithy, two inns, a windmill on the rise to the north-west.
- **Whittington Barracks**, a mile south-west on the Lichfield road, at 342 feet on the heath. The depot, the armoury, the guardroom.
- **Whittington Heath**, between village and barracks: the golf course by 1938.
- **Packington Hall** and **Little Packington**, south of the barracks, in their own trees.
- **Hademore**, where the railway crosses the road at a level crossing east of the canal, with **Brook Leasow** and the Leasow Brook running to the Tame.

## Tamhorn and Hopwas Hays

- **Tamhorn** and **Tamhorn Park**, a farm and an old park on the east side, between the railway and the Tame. Empty country, good for a boat that wants not to be seen.
- **Hopwas Hays Wood** rises on the west bank to 393 feet. The canal runs along its foot for a mile with the wood above it. The range butts, the red flags, the paths. **Woodhouse** is the keeper's house on the wood's south edge.
- **The Devil's Dressing Room**, a hollow in the hillside south-west of the wood, above Hopwas. A name a village gives a place it does not go.
- **Packington water works**, on the hill west of Hopwas.

## Hopwas (home)

- **The village** on the west bank where the road from Lichfield to Tamworth crosses: church, post office, smithy, an inn on the canal bridge and another on the road, Hopwas Farm.
- **The paper mill and weir** on the River Tame just below the village, where the river runs a hundred yards from the canal. Noise, water, a night shift.
- **Coton**, a hamlet a quarter mile north-east across the Tame with a hall and **Hill Head** behind.
- **Comberford** and **Comberford Hall**, a mile north-east: a mill on the Tame, old money, a lonely road.
- **The Alders**, **Millfield House** and **Broad Meadow** between Hopwas and Tamworth, the water meadows of the Tame.
- **Askew's mooring** is south of the village under the wood's last trees, where the towpath has no reason to go and the flags are in sight.

## Hopwas to Fazeley (the Birmingham and Fazeley pound)

- The map names this stretch the **Birmingham Canal**; it is the Birmingham and Fazeley company's water, which is why the toll house at Fazeley cares about it.
- **Dunstall Farm** on the west bank, then **Bonehill Cottage**, **Kendall's Wood** and **The Bodnets** on the rising ground to the west. Small woods, good for a walk nobody sees.
- **Bangley House** and **Bangley Farm** on the Watling Street side, and **Mile Oak Farm** at the mile post.
- **Bonehill**, a hamlet on the Tame with a mill, **Bonehill Lodge** and **Bonehill House**, between the canal and Tamworth.
- **Bitterscote** and **Lady Bridge**, the last fields before Tamworth, and **Tamworth Castle** above the river a mile and a half from Fazeley.

## Fazeley Junction (the south gate)

- **The junction**: the Coventry Canal and the Birmingham and Fazeley meet under the roving bridge, with the toll house on the corner and Peel's Wharf beside it.
- **Watling Street** crosses just south: the A5 by 1938, the lorries' road.
- **The village**: church, inn, telegraph, and two **tape mills** on the Bourne Brook, the Peel mills, one of them Tolson's four storeys of brick.
- **Fazeley Mill** on the Tame, and the **Tape Mill** ponds behind the village.
- **Drayton Manor park** on the west edge: lodges, the Decoy pond, the trees, and the space where the house stood until 1929. Empty parkland a rifle's length from the wharf.
- **Drayton Bassett**, a mile south-west, church and post office, the way south by the Birmingham and Fazeley towards Curdworth.
- **Two Gates** and **Kettlebrook** east on the Coventry Canal, with a paper mill and weir on the Tame, then **Glascote** and its two locks, the first locks of the whole game, and **Tamworth** station on the main line.

## What this gives the game

Two railways and one road run the length of the pound beside the water: the Trent Valley line east of the canal, the Lichfield to Tamworth road through Hopwas, and Watling Street at the bottom. Every mile has a mill, a farm or a hall, and between them there is a lot of empty riverside. The wood is the only high ground on the water. The Tame runs alongside the whole way, lower than the cut, a second road that only a swimmer uses.
