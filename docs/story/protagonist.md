# Askew

**Frank Askew.** Forty-one in 1936. Known on the cut as Askew, at the barracks as Sergeant, in the Tame Otter as the bloke off the boat. Nobody uses Frank.

## What is true

- Born 1895 in the Potteries. Enlisted 1914, Staffordshire Regiment. A rifleman, then a marksman, then by 1918 a sniper, though the word was not used in front of him. Stayed on after the war as a musketry instructor. Discharged 1932 on a thin pension when the army stopped needing men who could teach shooting to boys who would never shoot anyone.
- Came back to the only ground he knew: the range in Hopwas Hays Wood, where he had put ten thousand recruits through their course. Bought a tired ex-company motor with his gratuity and moored it at Hopwas, on the village side, where he can see the flags.
- The boat is *Halcyon*. A 1912 Josher motor with a single-cylinder Bolinder that has to be started with a blowlamp and reversed by stopping it and starting it backwards. He did not name her. The name is still on the cabin side under the grime, and he has never painted it out.
- He shoots on the butts when the sergeant-major looks away. The sergeant-major looks away because Askew taught him.
- He owns three guns. A service rifle he should not have. A sporting rifle with a scope that he bought legally and that every constable in the county has an opinion about. A pistol he does not talk about. All three live in a locker the size of a cupboard.

## What he wants

To be left alone on the boat. That is the whole of it, and he cannot afford it. The pension covers the diesel or the food, not both, so he takes work. Bounty for a deserter. A stolen load found. A towpath watched. A man persuaded to leave a pound. He is not a policeman, and he will not do what a gang would do, and every job he takes sits in the strip of water between those two banks.

He would tell you he takes the work for the money. He takes it because on the cut he is a man who is good at something, and in the village he is a man with a long gun and no wife.

## What he will not say

He does not volunteer war stories. He does not answer questions about the war. When he is drunk he is quieter, not louder. The series honours this: one true thing about the war per chapter, never told by him, always found. See the rule in [series.md](series.md).

What the writers know and the player does not: in the last month of the war he was ordered to shoot a man on his own side, and he did it, and the officer who gave the order is alive and on the rise. This is the deepest water in the series and it is not reached before Game 3.

## How he reads

- Speaks little. Complete sentences when he does. No slang he did not bring back from the army.
- Watches hands, exits, and the water level.
- Polite to boat families, women, and anyone in a uniform lower than sergeant. Short with clerks. Silent with officers.
- Does not drink on the boat. Drinks in the Tame Otter, one seat, back to the wall.
- Cabin: oil, wet wool, gun solvent, a wireless he keeps tuned to the dance bands and turns off when the news comes on. Roses and castles under the soot on the cabin doors, painted by a woman he never met for a family that sold up.

## What the player can and cannot make him do

The player chooses the jobs, the route, the method and the mercy. The player cannot make him take company livery, run a fleet, join a gang, join the police, or explain himself. When a choice would make him a different man, the game does not offer it.
