# Rooks and Rats: the farm job

The first job of the demo, written from James's brief of 21 October 2026: *he finds a farm, speaks to the owner, who asks him to take out some corvids and rats on his farm; if he can get them all the farmer gives him the contact of someone with potentially more work in a pub further down the canal.* James settled the gun on 23 October 2026: Askew carries a rook rifle from the start, so the job no longer gives him one; it pays with the lead. It is built and playable; it sits beside the demo's six missions until its place in the order is settled.

## Where

**Dunstall Farm**, on the west bank of the Birmingham and Fazeley pound, 1,750 metres south of Hopwas bridge, the first farm the map names below Hopwas. The stackyard stands forty-odd metres back from the water with its gate towards the cut: a post-and-rail fence, two thatched hayricks, a muck heap. Rooks and crows sit on the ricks and the posts and shift between them; a magpie works the heap; rats run between the rick feet and the muck. Askew lies *Sparrow* against the off-side bank, steps ashore and walks up to the gate.

Before the farm, **the paddock at Hopwas**: a hedged pasture on the towpath side, fifty yards south of the bridge, between the canal and the lane, with an open gate towards the water, a stone trough and three oaks standing in its hedge. Eight crows and two rooks feed on the grass and sit on the gate, the trough and the oaks. It is where he first fires the rifle, with nothing riding on it: the crows there count for nobody.

## Who

**Pryce**, the farmer, stands inside the gate and turns to watch a man who comes up from the water. He is not in the demo's cast and need not outlive this job.

## How it plays

1. **The offer.** Within a few yards of Pryce the prompt reads *Talk to the farmer*. He says: *You off the boat? Thought so. Nobody else walks up here.* Then: *That's a rook rifle you've got there. I've rooks in the stackyard and rats under the ricks, and no time to shoot them.* Then: *Clear the lot, every one of them, and I'll put you on to a man who pays for shooting.* The card at the top of the screen reads *Rooks and rats: 0 of 10*.
2. **The cull.** Z (or the left trigger) shoulders the rifle, and again lowers it: the view goes down the sights, from his eye on the stock to the foresight, a ring sits in the middle of the screen, the mouse alone lays the aim. Space, the left button or the right trigger fires. A single-shot rook rifle: one crack, the bolt worked, a second and a half before the next. X (or the d-pad) changes to the sporting rifle slung on his back, and the view goes through its telescope instead: the picture four times the size in a round black field with crosswires, five shots six tenths of a second apart, then a pause while a fresh magazine goes in. Six corvids (three rooks, two crows, a magpie) and four rats. A shot that meets a rick, a post or the ground first stops there. Every shot sends the birds within earshot up off their perches, calling, to settle somewhere else; a bird that is hit falls where it was and lies where it lands. The count climbs on the card; when the last falls it reads *All of them. Tell the farmer.* Talk to Pryce before then and he says *Not yet. I can still hear them.*
3. **The lead.** Pryce: *Every one of them. I've not seen shooting like that since France.* *There's a man drinks at the Three Tuns at Fazeley, name of Holland, agent at Tolson's mill. He asked after a man who could shoot.* *Ring Fazeley 14 and say Pryce sent you. Further down the cut, past the junction. You can't miss the mill.* The card now carries the lead and keeps it: **Holland · the Three Tuns, Fazeley · Fazeley 14**.

## What it hands on

Holland is the mill agent of [characters.md](characters.md), the bank-sider with work for a man who can find things, so the lead points at the demo's Fazeley end. The number is a 1938 exchange number: the town's name and two figures, the kind a farmer would read off a card by the telephone.

## The guns

A rook rifle: the farmer's gun of the period, a light single-shot sporting rifle for rooks and rabbits, .300 Rook, open sights, a box of fifty. It is Askew's own, carried in the open at the trail whenever he is ashore and stowed below when he steps aboard.

Beside it, since James's ask of 24 October 2026 (*make the rifle better, like a better scope and more ammo and faster reload, add another rifle that is like this*), the sporting rifle of [protagonist.md](protagonist.md): the one he bought legally and every constable in the county has an opinion about, here a bolt-action .22 rimfire repeater with a five-shot magazine and a four-power telescope sight, on his certificate, with a box of a hundred. Whichever he is not holding is slung across his back; X changes them over. Through the telescope the view is four times the size, in a round black field with crosswires, and the shots come six tenths of a second apart until the magazine is empty.

Neither is yet the hidden weapon of [systems.md](systems.md), which comes apart into the stock, the barrel and the scope and lives where no search would find it; how the openly carried sporting rifle squares with that is still to be settled. The pound does not yet hear the shot, though the birds do.

## What was settled

[protagonist.md](protagonist.md) has Askew owning three guns already, and the demo's first mission teaches the rifle on the range. James's call of 23 October 2026: he has the rook rifle from the start, the gun he carries openly while the real guns stay hidden, so the farm job pays with Pryce's word and the lead, not a gun. What is still open is where the job sits in the demo's order.
