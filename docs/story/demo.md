# The Pound: the demo

Autumn 1936, sixth week of the strike. Seven miles of water, one man, one boat, one boy who ran. Six missions, each a job with a client and a fee, together the story of why the pound is shut. Played in order; the water between them is open to roam.

## 1. Red Flags

*Client: nobody. Fee: nothing.*

The cold open (see [opening.md](opening.md)). Learn the boat on the pound: start, steer, moor, the wash complaint from a moored family that costs a favour if ignored. Learn the wood: red flags mean no landing on the wood bank, the paths, the butts. Learn the rifle on the range while Garner looks away: breathing, range, the scope, the sound carrying. Ends with Garner's job. The player now knows the pound has two ends and both are shut.

## 2. The Deserter

*Client: Garner, for the barracks. Fee: army rate, cash, no paper.*

Find Private Rudd. He is in the Hays, in a hide Askew himself dug for training in 1928, which the player finds by walking the wood Askew's way. Rudd has the rifle and will not point it. He ran because the armourer made him sign for a crate count that was short, and the men who came for the crates wore company boat clothes and came at night to the range track, where a lorry cannot go but a boat can.

The choice: walk him back to the guardroom for the fee, put him on a north-road boat and lie to Garner, or take the rifle and let him decide. All three pay differently and all three are remembered. The rifle stays with the player in every ending, because the book says it never existed.

## 3. The Dumped Load

*Client: Ada Skerritt. Fee: a favour, and the cut's goodwill.*

The Skerritts' coal was dumped off the range track two nights ago by a man they hired to watch it. Recover it, and find him. The coal was hiding crates, which is why it was moved, and the crates are not there now. The man is in the Red Lion spending money he should not have. This mission teaches what the boat families are for: Lil knows the man's face, Ada knows his family, Reuben knows which lorry he was seen getting into. Bring the load back and the Skerritts are the player's; bring it back and keep a crate and Ada knows.

Ends with one crate opened on *Halcyon*'s cabin floor. Service rifles, depot stamped, grease still soft.

## 4. The Man from the Tame Otter

*Client: Nell Cobbett. Fee: a paid slate and a room's worth of listening.*

A man drank in the Otter for three nights, paid in new notes, and left with her takings on the fourth. Last seen walking the towpath south. Watch the towpath at night; follow him to Fazeley. This is the town hub opening: Tench at the toll house, who denies the man and knows him; Holland at the mill, who has a job of his own; Peel's Wharf at two in the morning, where a company motor with no lamps ties up and Dunmore's lorry backs down to the water. The man is Dunmore's, and the takings were a bribe he was meant to give Ansell and drank instead.

Ends with the player having seen the crates cross from water to road, and Sergeant Vokes on the A5 bridge above, seeing the player.

## 5. The Dead Arm

*Client: Maud Bagnall. Fee: nothing she can pay. She asks anyway.*

Something has been moving in the arm at Huddlesford at night, past her cottage, past the planks. Walk the arm. The sunk boat in the reeds is not twenty years sunk; it was scuttled in September, and it is where the crates are kept between depot and wharf. The eerie beat: reeds, a boat with a name painted out, the north road boatmen in the Plough saying the west is worse, that Ogley is dry, that Lichfield is draining. The rumours of the west are Game 1's north map in a sentence each.

Ends with Askew in the cabin of the scuttled boat, finding Ansell's strike fund receipts and Tench's hand on all of them.

## 6. The Junction

*Client: Askew. Fee: the water.*

The player knows: the strike was let run, the pound is a yard, the money is Dunmore's, the books are Tench's, the guns are the depot's and are going south. Garner is told, or is not. Ansell is told, and the strike breaks in a night, or is not, and the player takes a windlass to the stop-planks at Fazeley under Vokes's lamp. Either way *Halcyon* goes under the roving bridge at dawn with the crate under the coal and Rudd's rifle in the locker.

Last shot: the chain across the junction, cut, sinking. Cut to the trailer for *The Cut*: Glascote's two locks, the black water of Birmingham, a Grand Union motor in new paint, the Thames.

## The demo's fence

- Water: Huddlesford Junction to Fazeley Junction only. Walkable: the Hays, the barracks approach, Fazeley's streets to the A5.
- The obstructions are things: a chain, stop-planks, a pair of boats tied across. The player can look at them, touch them, and in mission 6, remove one.
- Heat is local: see [systems.md](systems.md). There is nowhere to run to, which is the point.
- Every mission can be finished without a shot. Every mission is quicker with one, and the pound hears it.
