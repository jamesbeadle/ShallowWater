# The systems the story leans on

Only the systems the story needs are here. The rest is design.

## Heat on a linear canal

There are two directions and three miles an hour. Nobody escapes on the water, so heat is not a chase. Heat is **who knows**.

| Level | Name | What it means | How it spreads |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Talk | Somebody saw something | Along the towpath at boat speed, pub to pub, family to family |
| 2 | Watch | A picket, a keeper or a clerk is looking for the boat | Every moored boat passed reports it; every bridge is a pair of eyes |
| 3 | Bridge | The police are at a road bridge ahead | Faster than the boat: the road crosses the water every mile or two |
| 4 | Hunt | Vokes is on the bank with men | Only ever at a junction, a wharf or a bridge. Never on the water |

Heat drops with time, distance, a favour spent, or a boat family speaking for the player. It never drops by outrunning anyone. A shot fired raises it two levels at once on the whole pound. The boat families are a modifier on everything: a player they like is warned; a player they do not is reported.

In the demo, heat has nowhere to go. Vokes at Fazeley and the pickets at Hopwas make the seven miles a room, and that is the pressure of *The Pound*.

## The boat

*Halcyon* is the player's car and it is a bad one on purpose. Single-cylinder Bolinder: started with a blowlamp, run at one beat a second, no reverse gear, reversed by stopping the engine and restarting it backwards. The engine is a character. Breakdowns, a fouled propeller, a cold morning that will not start. Fuel is diesel and diesel is money.

Wash complaints are a social system, not a physics one: pass a moored family too fast and the cut hears. A butty, later, doubles the load and halves the handling. A horse boat is the poor option: quiet, slow, needs the towpath, and a Number One will lend one to a friend.

Night running is the freedom: no keepers, no clerks, no pickets. Night running is also how the guns move, and the player is doing what the villains do.

## Tone

Not cosy heritage. Not gangster. Cramped, smoky, proud, slightly doomed. The working cut in the last decade it worked. Jazz on a wireless, dole on the bank, roses and castles under the soot. The violence is short and it costs. Nobody says the word "canal" who lives on it: it is the cut.

## Art references

- FMC Josher motors, 1912 to 1925, with the Bolinder, in FMC's dark green and red, under twenty years of coal dust.
- The GUCCC Star, Town and Royalty classes, 1934 to 1937, in new paint, the villains' boats and the future.
- Working cabins: roses and castles, brass, lace, the range, the stove pipe.
- Hopwas Hays Wood in October: oak, the range butts, red flags, the firing point.
- Whittington Barracks: the 1880s red brick, the keep, the guardroom, the gate.
- Fazeley: Tolson's mill, Peel's Wharf, the roving bridge, the toll house, the Watling Street bridge.
- Huddlesford: the toll cottage, reeds, a sunk boat, stop-planks with weed on them.
- The A5 at night: Leyland and Albion lorries, headlamps, a yard with a lamp.
- Birmingham: Gas Street, Farmer's Bridge, the loops, the Black Country works.

## Sound references

- The single beat of a Bolinder, slow and heavy. The whole game keeps time to it.
- Rifle fire coming down a hill, flat, with the delay.
- Dance bands and the BBC on a wireless with a bad valve. The news read in received pronunciation and turned off.
- Lorries on Watling Street through the night.
- A horse's shoes on a towpath, a rope creaking on a stud, water in reeds, a lock leaking through a gate nobody is tending.
- Pubs: a piano, an argument about the strike, quiet when Askew comes in.
